Deconstructing Induction Circuits in Transformers: An ARENA Implementation Walkthrough

By Gaurav Katare • October 2026 • 12 min read • ARENA Mechanistic Interpretability

One of the most foundational discoveries in mechanistic interpretability—pioneered by Anthropic and central to the ARENA (Alignment Research Engineer Accelerator) curriculum—is the discovery of induction heads.

Induction heads explain how transformers perform in-context learning without updating weights. They are specialized attention circuits that complete repeating patterns:

[A][B] ... [A] ➔ [B]

While a 1-layer attention-only transformer can only implement bigram statistics (matching token [A] directly to words that frequently follow it in training data), a 2-layer transformer can form an induction circuit by composing attention heads across layers.

1. The Two-Head Circuit Architecture

The induction circuit consists of two distinct heads operating across consecutive layers:

  • Layer 0 Previous-Token Head (H₀): This head attends from token position i to position i-1. Its OV-circuit copies information about the preceding token into the residual stream at the current position. Thus, at position [B], the residual stream now contains the information: "I was preceded by [A]".
  • Layer 1 Induction Head (H₁): When the sequence later repeats token [A] at the current position curr, the query from [A] looks for keys that signal "preceded by [A]". The QK-circuit directs attention precisely back to token [B]. Then, H₁'s OV-circuit reads token [B] and writes a logit boost for [B] into the output distribution.

2. Verifying Induction Heads with TransformerLens

In ARENA Module 1, we implement this using Neel Nanda's TransformerLens library. By feeding a repeated random token sequence into a 2-layer model, induction heads immediately manifest as sharp off-diagonal bands in the attention pattern:

import torch from transformer_lens import HookedTransformer # Load 2-layer toy attention-only model model = HookedTransformer.from_pretrained("attn-only-2l") # Generate repeated random token sequence: [seq_len] repeated twice seq_len = 50 rand_tokens = torch.randint(100, 10000, (1, seq_len)) rep_tokens = torch.cat([rand_tokens, rand_tokens], dim=-1) # Run model and cache attention patterns logits, cache = model.run_with_cache(rep_tokens) # Inspect attention from second occurrence to token following first occurrence # Head L1H4 displays high attention strictly at offset -(seq_len - 1) attn_matrix = cache["blocks.1.attn.hook_pattern"][0, 4] # Layer 1, Head 4

3. Ablation and Direct Logit Attribution

To prove that this circuit causally drives the next-token prediction rather than merely correlating with it, we perform causal ablation. By zeroing out the attention pattern of Head 1.4 or replacing its activation with mean activations across the dataset, the model's loss on the second half of the sequence spikes dramatically, confirming that Head 1.4 is causally responsible for the in-context copy mechanism.

4. Implications for AI Safety

Why do induction heads matter for safety?

First, the transition when induction heads form during training corresponds to a macroscopic "phase change" in loss curves—a sudden jump in capability. Understanding the mechanistic circuits behind capability jumps is critical to predicting when models might abruptly acquire dangerous capabilities (such as autonomous replication, hacking, or strategic deception).

Second, understanding how information routes through transformer residual streams enables activation patching and circuit disruption, laying the groundwork for verifiable safety monitors that inspect a model's computational graph in real time.
